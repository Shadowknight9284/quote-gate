\section{Problem and contribution}
Under a declared \emph{synthetic} market model, does a displayed-quantity imbalance signal from one
reference venue carry predictive information, and does any advantage survive the spread, fees,
execution delay and venue failures? QuoteGate contributes (i) a small distributed market-data system
(venue simulators, persistent TCP feeds, an aggregator with per-venue sessions, sequence ordering and
local-receipt freshness) and (ii) a transparent target-position policy evaluated with integer,
reconciled execution accounting. Nothing here is evidence about real markets.

{\footnotesize The ledger's confirmed position feeds back to the strategy (not drawn).}
The strategy observes only the reference venue A's accepted bid/ask prices and displayed quantities,
A's locally observed freshness and connection state, and its own \emph{confirmed} position and order
state. It never sees future quotes, simulator draws, the latent price, future outcomes, or results.
Freshness is elapsed local time since receipt (50\,ms), not source age.

\section{Signal and policy}
For A's displayed sizes $b_t,a_t\ge 1$, $I_t=(b_t-a_t)/(b_t+a_t)$, computed exactly in integer basis
points. This is one venue's top-of-book displayed-size imbalance, not trade-flow imbalance. With
$\theta=0.2$ and $q_{\max}=5$ (frozen before evaluation), the target is $q^*_t=+q_{\max}$ if
$I_t>\theta$, $-q_{\max}$ if $I_t<-\theta$, else $0$ (equality maps to $0$). One decision per step
sends $\Delta q_t=q^*_t-q_t$ (clipped to 5) where $q_t$ is the confirmed position. No order is sent
while A is stale or invalid (the position is held, not assumed closed), while an order is outstanding,
or while any outcome is \emph{unknown}.

\section{Synthetic market and execution}
A latent price $p_t$ (integer ticks) drives A's quotes $[p_t,p_t+1]$; venue B quotes at or outside A,
so there is no cross-venue arbitrage. Sizes are uniform on $\{1..100\}$. With probability $m=0.5$ the
price moves one tick, up with probability $(1+\beta I_t)/2$: $\beta=0$ is the \emph{null} regime,
$\beta=0.5$ the \emph{controlled} regime, an illustrative assumption. $I_t$ is observable at step $t$
and affects only $p_{t+1}-p_t$ (verified by test). Market randomness is independent of faults and of
the strategy, so every configuration sees the identical quote stream per seed.

Orders are immediate-or-cancel limits at the observed price (buy at ask, sell at bid), routed to one
venue. On arrival the venue checks session, current executable price (buy fills at ask $\le$ limit,
sell at bid $\ge$ limit) and displayed size, then fills fully or rejects. A fill consumes displayed
size until the venue's next quote. A lost acknowledgement makes the outcome \emph{unknown}: nothing
is resent or booked until reconciliation by client order ID. Accounting uses
$c\leftarrow c-p\,\Delta q-f$, $q\leftarrow q+\Delta q$ with FIFO realized P\&L; duplicate fill IDs
are no-ops and conflicting reuse is rejected.

\section{Experiments}
Thirty unseen seeds (101--130), 5{,}000 steps each, fee $f=1$ tick per unit (1 tick $=0.01$ currency
units, declared). Cells: (1) no trading; (2) null; (3) controlled; (4) controlled with a 2-step
execution delay and 1-step acknowledgement delay; (5) controlled with faults (A's feed silent 50 of
every 500 steps; 5\% of acknowledgements lost). Economics use the evaluator-authoritative ledger rebuilt from venue fill records (never shown to the
strategy). \emph{Net} is the post-fee terminal liquidation-value P\&L: cash plus open inventory valued at
the last fresh A executable price (bid if long, ask if short) minus a fee for that \emph{hypothetical}
liquidation (no trade occurs); \emph{gross} (pre-fee) = net + all fees. Values are per-seed means $\pm$ sample standard deviation over 30 seeds,
in ticks$\times$units. The \emph{hit rate} is the share of steps with $|I_t|>\theta$ and a price move
where $\operatorname{sign}(I_t)=\operatorname{sign}(p_{t+1}-p_t)$ (49{,}431 such steps); it is computed by the
evaluator and is directional accuracy, not profitability.

\section{Interpretation}
The planted information is detectable (hit rate .633 vs .502) and worth a paired $+1592\pm107$ ticks
on every seed, but the policy loses on every seed in every regime. In the null regime the gross loss
($-8974$) matches the half-spread paid when crossing ($\approx 8992$), as expected without drift.
\textbf{Break-even.} A round trip of one unit pays the spread $s=1$ tick and two fees $2f$. Holding for
$H$ steps earns about $m(2P-1)H\approx 0.5\cdot0.27\,H\approx0.13H$ ticks, where $P\approx0.63$ is
the conditional up-probability. Because $I_t$ is redrawn every step, positions turn over every
$\sim$1.5 steps, so $0.13H\ll s+2f=3$: losses are expected. Under delay, losses are smaller only
because fewer orders fill (1109 sent per seed, 37.5\% rejected, mostly because the price had moved);
the 2-step delay exceeds the 1-step signal horizon and loss per unit traded worsens. Lower losses
are not better execution. Under faults the policy held through 449 stale steps and halted for 1004
steps on 100 unknown outcomes per seed; after reconciliation every venue fill was booked exactly
once except 11 fills belonging to orders unresolved at run end (8 unknown, the rest with
acknowledgements in flight): the strategy-known ledger then differs from the authoritative one on
11 seeds (total net difference 20 ticks), and results use the authoritative ledger. 21 of 30 seeds end
with open inventory, valued hypothetically as above.

\section{Systems result (separate population)}
The same policy, order protocol and ledger also ran over the networked transport (one seed, 10\,s
admission window): venue on a second VM on the same LAN, aggregator and strategy on the first.
In a normal run all 3012 orders filled and were acknowledged; with every 50th acknowledgement
dropped, 21 orders became unknown, trading paused, and all 21 were recovered by status query
($\approx$302\,ms), never resent. The ledger matched the venue's fill log exactly; position never
exceeded 5. Order-to-acknowledgement round trip (application level, one host's clock) was
649\,$\mu$s median versus 132\,$\mu$s with both processes on one VM. These figures are not part of
the table above.

\section{Limitations}
Results hold only for this synthetic model, these frozen parameters and these 30 seeds; they are not
a general impossibility result and not evidence of real-market alpha. Fills ignore queues, impact and
partial fills; these results come from the in-process backtest, not from the networked venue transport;
measured system latencies (feed and route round trips) are separate from the simulated delays here.
