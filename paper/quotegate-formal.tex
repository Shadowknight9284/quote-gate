\section*{Inspiration}

When a trading system loses money, people usually blame the strategy. Many losses come from
the plumbing instead:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=0pt]
  \item an order executes but its acknowledgement never arrives;
  \item a feed freezes, and the system keeps acting on a price hundreds of milliseconds old;
  \item a duplicate execution report books the same fill twice;
  \item the strategy believes it is flat while the venue shows it holding inventory.
\end{itemize}
None of these are alpha problems; they are correctness problems. A backtest that assumes perfect
fills and instant acknowledgements never shows them.

We wanted a system where each of those failures happens on purpose, is handled by an explicit
rule, and leaves records anyone can reconcile afterwards. We started from one question:
\emph{what should a trading system do when it does not know?} Our answer is that it never
guesses.

%==========================================================================
\section*{What it does}

QuoteGate is an end-to-end simulated trading workflow that excludes stale quotes, bounds
positions, and reconciles venue fills with accounting. This section defines each stage
formally, then reports results.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Market data.}

\begin{definition}[Venues and quotes]
Let $\Venues$ be a finite, totally ordered set of venue identifiers (in the experiments,
$\Venues = \{A, B\}$, with $A$ the reference venue). A quote from venue $v$ is
\[
  Q = (v,\ \sigma,\ n,\ b,\ a,\ s^b,\ s^a),
\]
with symbol $\sigma$, sequence number $n \in \Z_{\ge 1}$, bid and ask prices $b, a$ in integer
ticks, and displayed sizes $s^b, s^a \in \Z_{\ge 1}$. All prices and quantities are integers.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quote validity]
A quote is \emph{valid} if its fields are well typed and in range, $s^b, s^a \ge 1$, and
$a > b$. A locked quote ($a = b$) counts as crossed and is invalid. Invalid quotes, and quotes
with the wrong symbol or venue, invalidate the venue's current state
(\code{book.py}, \code{INVALIDATING}).
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Sources.}
The primary source is a synthetic market (defined below) streamed by venue
simulators. The extension source is a 600-second capture of Coinbase Exchange's public BTC-USD
\code{level2\_batch} feed, rebuilt into an L2 book in exact integer units ($0.01$~USD ticks,
$10^{-8}$~BTC size units). The capture is read-only: no account, key or order entry exists in
the code.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Venue state and freshness gating.}

The aggregator's book (\code{book.py}) keeps one state per venue. It is pure and clock-free:
every time-dependent call takes an injected monotonic time $t$ in nanoseconds.

\begin{definition}[Venue state]
For each venue $v$ the book keeps a connection flag $c_v$, a connection epoch $e_v$
(incremented on every connect), the last accepted sequence $n_v$ (reset to $0$ on connect), the
last accepted quote $Q_v$, its local receipt time $r_v$, and an invalid flag $\iota_v$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Acceptance.}
A quote $Q$ arriving with epoch $e$ is accepted if and only if
\[
  e = e_v \ \wedge\ c_v \ \wedge\ Q \text{ valid} \ \wedge\ n > n_v .
\]
A quote with $n = n_v$ is rejected as a duplicate and one with $n < n_v$ as out of order. A
quote from a superseded epoch is rejected as \code{old\_epoch}. Accepting a quote sets
$Q_v \leftarrow Q$, $r_v \leftarrow t$, $n_v \leftarrow n$. If $n > n_v + 1$, a gap is counted
but the quote is still accepted.

\begin{definition}[Feed status]
With freshness threshold $\tau > 0$, the status of venue $v$ at time $t$ is
\[
  \mathrm{status}_v(t) =
  \begin{cases}
    \textsc{Disconnected} & \neg c_v,\\
    \textsc{Invalid}      & c_v \wedge \iota_v,\\
    \textsc{NoQuote}      & c_v \wedge \neg\iota_v \wedge Q_v = \varnothing,\\
    \textsc{Stale}        & \text{otherwise, if } t - r_v \ge \tau,\\
    \textsc{Fresh}        & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
The boundary is inclusive: a quote is stale at exactly $t - r_v = \tau$. The experiments use
$\tau = 50$~ms. The networked market-data runs use $\tau = 100$~ms.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
Freshness is elapsed \emph{local} time since receipt, not age at the source. A quote received
just now may have been delayed arbitrarily before it arrived. The system claims nothing about
source-to-receiver latency.
\end{remark}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Signal and information set.}

For the reference venue's displayed sizes $s^b_t, s^a_t \ge 1$, the top-of-book displayed-size
imbalance is computed exactly in integer basis points, truncating toward zero:
\begin{equation}
  I_t \;=\; \operatorname{trunc}\!\left( 10^4 \cdot \frac{s^b_t - s^a_t}{s^b_t + s^a_t} \right)
  \ \text{bp} \;\in\; [-10^4, 10^4].
\end{equation}
The signal is defined only when $\mathrm{status}_A(t) = \textsc{Fresh}$.

The strategy observes only (i) $A$'s accepted prices and displayed sizes, (ii) $A$'s locally
observed freshness and connection state, and (iii) its own \emph{confirmed} position and order
state. It never observes future quotes, simulator draws, the latent price, venue-side records,
or evaluation results.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Synthetic market model.}

The model below is an illustrative assumption, not a market finding.

\begin{definition}[Market]
A latent price $p_t \in \Z$ (ticks) evolves in discrete steps of 10~ms. Venue $A$ quotes
$[p_t,\ p_t + 1]$, a one-tick spread. Venue $B$ quotes at or outside $A$, so there is no
cross-venue arbitrage. Displayed sizes are i.i.d.\ uniform on $\{1,\dots,100\}$. Each step:
\begin{equation}
  p_{t+1} - p_t =
  \begin{cases}
    +1 & \text{w.p. } m \cdot \tfrac{1 + \beta \hat I_t}{2},\\[2pt]
    -1 & \text{w.p. } m \cdot \tfrac{1 - \beta \hat I_t}{2},\\[2pt]
    \phantom{+}0 & \text{w.p. } 1 - m,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad m = 0.5,
\end{equation}
where $\hat I_t = I_t / 10^4 \in [-1, 1]$. The \emph{null} regime has $\beta = 0$ and the
\emph{controlled} regime has $\beta = 0.5$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Causality]
$I_t$ is observable at step $t$ and affects only $p_{t+1} - p_t$ (verified by test). Market
randomness comes from a generator separate from fault and execution randomness, and strategy
actions never change the market stream. For a fixed seed, every configuration within a regime
sees the identical quote stream.
\end{assumption}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Target-position policy.}

The parameters were frozen before evaluation: $\theta = 2000$~bp ($0.2$), $q_{\max} = 5$,
maximum order size $5$, cooldown $0$. The target position is
\begin{equation}
  q^\star_t =
  \begin{cases}
    +q_{\max} & I_t > \theta,\\
    -q_{\max} & I_t < -\theta,\\
    0         & |I_t| \le \theta \quad (\text{equality maps to } 0),
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and the order quantity is $\Delta q_t = \operatorname{clip}(q^\star_t - q_t,\ -5,\ 5)$, where
$q_t$ is the \emph{confirmed} position. There is one decision per step in the backtest and one
per accepted reference quote in the live runner.

\begin{definition}[Trading gate]
An order is sent at $t$ only if all of the following hold:
\[
  \mathrm{status}_A(t) = \textsc{Fresh}
  \ \wedge\ \text{no order outstanding}
  \ \wedge\ \text{no outcome } \textsc{Unknown}
  \ \wedge\ \Delta q_t \neq 0 .
\]
When $A$ is stale or invalid the position is \emph{held}, not assumed closed. There is no
emergency liquidation policy.
\end{definition}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Smart order routing.}

For side $d \in \{\text{buy}, \text{sell}\}$, quantity $Q \ge 1$, time $t$ and an optional
allowed set $\mathcal{A} \subseteq \Venues$, the eligible set is
\begin{equation}
  E_d(Q, t) = \bigl\{\, v \in \mathcal{A} :
     \mathrm{status}_v(t) = \textsc{Fresh}\ \wedge\ s_v^{d} \ge Q \,\bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $s_v^{\text{buy}} = s^a_v$ and $s_v^{\text{sell}} = s^b_v$. The router (\code{router.py})
selects
\begin{equation}
  v^\star =
  \begin{cases}
    \arg\min_{v \in E} a_v & d = \text{buy},\\
    \arg\max_{v \in E} b_v & d = \text{sell},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
breaking ties by the lowest venue identifier. If $E = \varnothing$ there is no route. Every
excluded venue is reported with its reason (its status, \code{insufficient\_quantity}, or
\code{not\_allowed}). Orders are never split.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Simulated execution with explicit uncertainty.}

Orders are immediate-or-cancel limits at the observed price: a buy is limited at the observed
ask and a sell at the observed bid. On arrival the venue checks the session, then fills a buy if
its current ask is at most the limit (a sell if its current bid is at least the limit) and its
displayed size covers the quantity. It fills fully or rejects with a reason
(\code{price\_moved}, \code{insufficient\_quantity}, \code{session}, \code{bad\_order},
\code{conflicting\_order\_id}, \code{store\_full}). A fill consumes displayed size until the
venue's next quote. There are no partial fills, resting orders or retries. The fee is
$f = 1$~tick per unit, with $1$~tick $= 0.01$ currency units.

\begin{invariant}[Record before acknowledge]
A venue records an order's outcome in its order store \emph{before} writing the execution
report. Execution and status frames are never coalesced or dropped. Only quotes coalesce, under
write backpressure.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Idempotency]
Resending a client order ID with an identical payload returns the stored outcome without
re-execution. A different payload under the same ID is rejected as
\code{conflicting\_order\_id}. The store holds at most $1024$ records, and an unreleased record
is never evicted.
\end{invariant}

\paragraph{Order-outcome state machine.}
Each order moves through the states below. Let $\eta$ be the ack timeout ($0.2$~s in the
cross-VM runs), and let \textit{inst} be the venue's per-process instance identifier.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
From & Event & To \\
\midrule
\textsc{Sent}    & exec report (matching venue, instance, session) & \textsc{Filled} / \textsc{Rejected} \\
\textsc{Sent}    & no exec within $\eta$, or disconnect while sent & \textsc{Unknown} \\
\textsc{Unknown} & status: \code{found=true}                        & \textsc{Filled} / \textsc{Rejected} \\
\textsc{Unknown} & status: \code{found=false}, same \textit{inst}   & \textsc{NotExecuted} \\
\textsc{Unknown} & status: \code{found=false}, new \textit{inst}    & \textsc{LostRecord} (stays halted) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent While any order is \textsc{Unknown} or \textsc{LostRecord}, no new order is sent.
Status queries are bounded (at most one in flight per order, at most $50$ per order), and the
order is \textbf{never resent}. The same-instance \code{found=false} case is sound because
records precede acknowledgements and unreleased records are never evicted. If the venue has
restarted (a new instance), its in-memory store is gone, so \code{found=false} is not proof
that the order never executed.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Exact accounting and reconciliation.}

For a booked fill of signed quantity $\Delta q$ at price $p$ with fee $f_{\text{fill}}$, the
ledger (\code{ledger.py}) applies
\begin{equation}
  \text{cash} \leftarrow \text{cash} - p\,\Delta q - f_{\text{fill}},
  \qquad
  q \leftarrow q + \Delta q,
\end{equation}
with FIFO lot matching for realized P\&L.

\begin{invariant}[Ledger]
\begin{enumerate*}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item A duplicate fill ID with identical content is a no-op.
  \item Conflicting reuse of a fill ID raises an error.
  \item A fill that would make $|q| > q_{\max}$ raises an error: the position bound is enforced
        inside the ledger, not only in the strategy.
\end{enumerate*}
\end{invariant}

Two ledgers are kept. The \emph{strategy-known} ledger contains the fills the strategy has
confirmed. The \emph{evaluator-authoritative} ledger replays every venue fill record and is
never shown to the strategy. They can differ only by outcomes still unresolved at the end of a
run. Economics use the authoritative ledger. Terminal valuation is \emph{hypothetical}: open
inventory is valued at the last fresh executable reference price (the bid if long, the ask if
short), less a fee for that liquidation. No liquidation trade occurs.

\begin{definition}[Reconciliation]
Let $F_{\text{venue}}$ be the multiset of venue fill records and $F_{\text{ledger}}$ the booked
fills. A run reconciles when $F_{\text{venue}} = F_{\text{ledger}}$, that is,
\[
  \#\text{mismatched} = \#\text{unbooked} = \#\text{extra} = 0 .
\]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Quant experiment (deterministic backtest).}
Thirty seeds unseen during development (101--130), $5000$ steps each. The five cells are:
(1)~no trading; (2)~null; (3)~controlled; (4)~controlled with a 2-step execution delay and
1-step acknowledgement delay; (5)~controlled with faults ($A$'s feed silent for 50 of every 500
steps, and $5\%$ of acknowledgements lost). Values are per-seed means $\pm$ sample standard
deviation, in ticks$\times$units. \emph{Gross} is pre-fee and \emph{Net} is post-fee terminal
liquidation-value P\&L. The hit rate is the share of steps with $|I_t| > \theta$ and a price
move where $\operatorname{sign}(I_t) = \operatorname{sign}(p_{t+1} - p_t)$. It measures
directional accuracy, not profitability.

\paragraph{Break-even analysis.}
A one-unit round trip costs the spread plus two fees, $s + 2f = 1 + 2 = 3$ ticks. With
conditional up-probability $P \approx 0.63$ given $I_t > \theta$, holding for $H$ steps earns
about
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[\text{gain}] \approx m\,(2P - 1)\,H \approx 0.5 \cdot 0.27 \cdot H \approx 0.13\,H
  \ \text{ticks per unit}.
\end{equation}
Because $I_t$ is redrawn every step, positions turn over every $H \approx 1.5$ steps, so
$0.13\,H \ll 3$ and losses are expected. Under delay, losses shrink only because fewer orders
fill ($37.5\%$ rejected); loss per unit traded worsens. \textbf{A detectable signal is not a
profitable one.} This is a negative result for this model, these frozen parameters and these
seeds only. It is not a general impossibility result and not evidence about real markets.

\section*{How we built it}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em, style=nextline, itemsep=2pt]
  \item[Language and dependencies] Python~3.12, standard library only, built on
        \code{asyncio}. Nothing to install on the runtime path.
  \item[Pure, clock-free core] Book, router, strategy, matcher and ledger take an injected
        \code{now\_ns}. Tests set time to the nanosecond, including exact staleness boundaries.
        The runtime uses \code{time.monotonic\_ns()}.
  \item[Transport] Newline-delimited JSON over persistent TCP, one connection per venue,
        frames $\le 4$~KiB. Quotes, pings, orders, execution reports and status queries share
        that connection with a single reader that demultiplexes them. Reconnect backoff starts
        at 50~ms and doubles to a 1~s cap.
  \item[Bounded memory] Frames, client connections ($\le 16$), outstanding orders ($1$),
        outstanding pings ($1$), the order store ($1024$) and sample rings are all bounded.
        Every drop increments a counter.
  \item[Two harnesses, one logic] A deterministic step-based backtest (with injected delays,
        dropped acks and outages) and a live asyncio runner share the book, strategy, router,
        matcher and ledger code.
  \item[Real data] Coinbase public WebSocket (\code{level2\_batch}), read through a minimal
        standard-library WebSocket client with TLS certificate verification, and rebuilt into
        an L2 book in exact integer units taken from the product metadata.
  \item[Operations console] A local browser console (bound to 127.0.0.1) over the same code.
        It shows venue health, route previews, fault injection, order lifecycle,
        reconciliation, and the experiment matrix.
  \item[Optional analytics] Post-run import into Tiger Cloud (TimescaleDB / PostgreSQL).
        Database percentiles matched local recomputation exactly (0~ns difference). It is never
        on the runtime path.
  \item[Testing and CI] \code{unittest} (224 tests) and GitHub Actions on every push and pull
        request.
  \item[Paper] \LaTeX{} with pgfplots.
  \item[Development tools] Claude Code (integration and review), Codex (an audit pass), and a
        local Hermes agent for small delegated patches. Every patch was reviewed and re-tested
        before it was applied, and several were rejected. No AI runs in the application.
\end{description}

\section*{Challenges we ran into}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Choosing what ``unknown'' means.} The hardest design question was not speed.
        It was what to do when a fill might have happened. Resending risks a double execution;
        assuming failure risks positions nobody is tracking. We chose the state machine above:
        halt, ask the venue, and if the venue has lost its memory, stay halted.
  \item \textbf{Defining freshness honestly.} We can only measure how long ago \emph{we}
        received a quote, not how old it was at the source. The definition of
        $\mathrm{status}_v(t)$ says so explicitly.
  \item \textbf{Keeping the claims as narrow as the evidence.} Every number is labelled
        human-run, agent-run, or recorded. Every measurement states exactly which clock and
        which code path it covers.
  \item \textbf{CI caught two real concurrency bugs.} A client sending a flood of requests could
        starve the feed reader, and shutdown could hang while a client was still connected.
        Both are fixed and covered by regression tests.
  \item \textbf{The paper review caught our own errors.} An inventory count and some wording
        about ``identical quote streams'' were wrong. We corrected the paper; the reproduced
        data did not change.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Accomplishments that we're proud of}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item A ledger that reconciles to \textbf{zero mismatched, zero unbooked, zero extra} fills
        against the venue, including under dropped acknowledgements and disconnects.
  \item Failure modes are \emph{tests}, not anecdotes: a lost ack after a fill, a disconnect
        between execution and acknowledgement, a venue restart that loses its records,
        duplicate execution reports, and conflicting reuse of an order ID.
  \item In the cross-VM dropped-ack run, all 21 unknown orders were reconciled by status query
        and none was resent. The position never exceeded its bound of 5.
  \item An experiment anyone can reproduce: rerunning the matrix matched the paper's source on
        every substantive field. Only the run ID differs.
  \item Reporting a negative result honestly instead of tuning until it disappeared.
  \item No dependencies: the full demo runs in seconds on any machine with Python~3.12.
\end{itemize}

%==========================================================================
\section*{What we learned}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item In trading infrastructure, ``I don't know'' is a valid state. The system goes wrong
        only if it treats \textsc{Unknown} as ``yes'' or ``no''.
  \item Latency numbers mean nothing until you say what is measured. Our $\approx 53\,\mu$s is
        one process over loopback; across VMs it was $\approx 649\,\mu$s. We publish both and
        explain why they are not comparable.
  \item Fees and spread are the first thing a backtest should model, not the last. The
        break-even inequality $0.13\,H \ll s + 2f$ decides the outcome before any tuning.
  \item A detectable signal is not a profitable one: hit rate and P\&L answer different
        questions.
\end{itemize}

\section*{What's next for Quote Gate}

\paragraph{Planned work.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Durable order state.} Persist the venue order store and the ledger so
        deduplication survives restarts.
  \item \textbf{Exchange sandbox.} Paper trading against a real exchange sandbox using the same
        \textsc{Unknown}/reconciliation logic.
  \item \textbf{Risk controls.} A kill switch, a maximum-loss limit, and an explicit, tested
        policy for flattening positions.
  \item \textbf{Richer execution.} Partial fills, queue position and market impact.
  \item \textbf{Scaling the aggregator.} Separate feed handling from strategy and client work.
        The single event loop is the expected next bottleneck, though not yet measured.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Current limitations.}

\begin{assumption}[Synthetic validity]
The quant results assume the declared synthetic model, in which the predictive relationship is
planted. They say nothing about whether such a signal exists in real markets.
\end{assumption}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Single coordinator.} One aggregator process holds all strategy and ledger
        state, and nothing is persisted. A restart loses positions, pending orders and
        counters.
  \item \textbf{In-memory venue store.} A venue restart loses its order records, so
        deduplication is not restart-safe. The state machine handles this by staying halted
        (\textsc{LostRecord}).
  \item \textbf{Execution model.} No partial fills, queue position, hidden liquidity or market
        impact.
  \item \textbf{Sample sizes.} Cross-VM results are one seed and one run per configuration.
        The real-data replay is one 10-minute capture, and \code{level2\_batch} has no sequence
        numbers, so message gaps cannot be detected in it.
  \item \textbf{Latency scope.} Neither latency figure is an exchange-grade claim, and the VMs
        may share a physical host.
\end{enumerate}
